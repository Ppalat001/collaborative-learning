# 04: How the AI suggestions work (plain-language explanation for participants)

> DRAFT for researcher and ethics review. Not approved participant text. It describes the behaviour of groups on `policy-v2-3` in the `proactive_adaptive` condition. The approved information sheet and consent form take precedence.

**What stays the same.** The tasks, the questions before and after the session, and the questionnaires are exactly the same as before. You can always ask for help yourself through Step 2, and nothing described here takes that away.

**The group facilitator.** Sometimes the AI facilitator offers your group a short suggestion about *how* to work together, for example a way to compare your views. It never writes your answer and never decides who is right.

- When your group is discussing well, including when you disagree but keep giving new reasons, the facilitator stays quiet.
- When a suggestion is available, a small notice appears under the chat. "Show me" takes you to it. "Hide" hides the notice just for you.
- If you dismiss a suggestion, it is hidden only for you. Others in your group can still open it.
- If anyone opens it, the whole group sees it.

**Private suggestions.** If you write that you are unsure, the AI may ask you, and only you, whether you would like a small hint or a guiding question.

- Nobody else in your group sees this.
- Nothing is sent to the AI unless you click Yes.
- It starts with the smallest useful help and never gives you the answer.
- You can say "Not now", say "No thanks", or choose "Don't suggest again in this scenario". If you choose the last one, you get no more of these suggestions in that scenario, and your own Step 2 help stays available.
- Pausing to think is normal. Being quiet on its own never leads to a suggestion.

**"Did this help you make progress?"** After you have seen a suggestion, you may get one optional question with the answers Yes, Partly, No, I didn't need it and Skip.

- It takes one click and you can ignore it.
- It is not part of the test.
- If you don't answer, nothing is counted against you.

**What the system learns about you.** For each person, the system keeps track of how often you wanted suggestions and which kind (a question, a hint or a short explanation) you said helped. It uses this to decide whether to offer a suggestion and which kind.

- If you managed a few tasks on your own, it offers less.
- If you said a small hint was not enough, it may offer a slightly fuller explanation next time, which you can still decline.
- It does not judge your ability, does not give you a grade, and does not assume you understood something just because you typed a lot.
- A suggestion you never saw, or did not answer, is not counted as a "no".

**What is recorded.** For research, the system records the following, under an anonymous ID and never your nickname:

- when a suggestion was created
- whether it appeared on your screen
- what you clicked
- whether you answered the optional one-click question
